# Learn-to-Earn

Its purpose is not to reward passive content consumption. A valid module should define what the user is expected to understand or demonstrate and how the result is assessed.

## Learning flow

1. The module states its objective, prerequisite, expected time, and reward.
2. The user receives explanation and examples appropriate to their current competency.
3. The user completes the required interaction, assessment, or supported practical exercise.
4. The system evaluates the result using published scoring and integrity rules.
5. The user receives feedback, a completion state, and — where eligible — XP, a credential, or access.

## Assessment states

* not started;
* in progress;
* submitted;
* passed;
* needs review;
* retry available;
* failed;
* invalidated;
* appealed;
* and superseded by a newer curriculum version.

## Integrity

Learn-to-Earn may use identity, timing, similarity, behavioural, deepfake, plagiarism, AI-generated-content, and manual-review signals. These controls are not interchangeable and must not be presented as perfect detection.

Vendor names should not be published until the exact integration, role, data transfer, retention, accuracy limits, and public naming rights are confirmed.

## Competency is bounded

Passing a module demonstrates performance against that module’s defined criteria. It does not establish general financial expertise, suitability, legal qualification, or guaranteed safe behaviour.
